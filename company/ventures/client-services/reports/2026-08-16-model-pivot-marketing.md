# Marketing report — model comparison after SES exclusion

Date: 2026-08-16  
Owner: Marketing

## Executive summary

**Recommendation: choose (B), a tightly scoped, non-SES B2B development offer, as the first 30-day validation hypothesis.** Do not market general "Web / app / DX / AI" capability. Start with one buyer and one observable workflow: for example, *small, non-SES service businesses that lose time and follow-up quality when enquiries and bookings are handled across phone, forms, messaging, and spreadsheets*. The initial product is a paid or price-tested **"enquiry / booking workflow diagnostic + fixed-scope prototype"**, not a bespoke system build.

This is a hypothesis, not evidence of demand. Compared with (A), it can obtain learning before product-market fit is known; compared with (C), it uses the stated development capabilities and does not require product sourcing, fulfilment, returns, or paid acquisition. Public launch, contacting prospects, pricing proposals, and any spend remain out of scope until explicit human approval.

## Findings

- Nordstern can provide Web production, Web development, and mobile-app development; it has no publishable track record.
- The founder can allocate 30–40 hours per week and the 30-day budget cap is JPY30,000.
- SES customers are prohibited and no employer customers, information, code, or other work assets may be used.
- The founder brief identifies real estate and nightlife businesses as sectors with contacts, but whether those contacts are independent of employment and usable is unknown. They must not be assumed as a channel.
- No evidence has been supplied for customer demand, willingness to pay, supplier access, product category, or a referral route. The older decision to target SES is invalid under the new constraint.

### Positioning and core message for B

Proposed positioning (hypothesis): **"小規模事業者の、問い合わせ・予約・顧客対応の手作業を、まず一つだけ見える化・整流化するIT担当。"**

Landing-page message prototype:

- Headline: **「問い合わせ・予約の『次に何をする？』を、ひとつの流れに。」**
- Subhead: **電話・フォーム・メッセージ・表計算に分かれた対応を確認し、固定範囲の試作まで設計します。大規模な入れ替えや自動送信は初期範囲に含めません。**
- CTA (approval needed before publishing): **「業務フロー診断の内容を見る」** rather than an unqualified "contact us".
- Proof discipline: label all work **"自主制作デモ／架空データ"**; make no savings, implementation-time, or AI-performance claim until measured.

The business type must be chosen after validating reachable, non-employer-derived audiences. Real-estate offices and appointment-led local services are candidate segments, not recommended facts.

### Low-cost channel strategy (all future external activity requires approval)

1. **Founder-led discovery via independently sourced, permissioned warm introductions:** lowest-cost route to learn decision language, but only after checking the contact is unrelated to employer assets and securing approval to reach out.
2. **A focused landing page plus a synthetic demo:** credibility and conversion destination for introductions; keep hosting/domain spending within a pre-approved budget.
3. **Search-led content:** one narrowly useful guide such as "問い合わせ対応フローの棚卸しシート" and an accompanying downloadable template. Publish only after approval. Search demand is unknown; treat SEO as compounding research, not a 30-day lead guarantee.
4. **Local-business communities / LinkedIn:** only test after the message is validated in interviews. Avoid broad social posting or paid ads in the initial 30 days.

Do not hire a commission-only sales agent or run ads in the first validation cycle: neither improves the missing problem proof, and both can create messaging and brand-risk before the offer is fixed.

### First marketing experiment: message and problem validation

Prepare (internally only):

- A one-page offer with the segment placeholder, workflow, included/excluded scope, synthetic-demo disclosure, and a no-commitment diagnostic request.
- Two message variants: (1) "対応漏れ・確認の手間を減らす" and (2) "予約から次の対応までを見える化する." 
- A five-question interview guide: current steps; frequency; time/rework; existing workaround; consequence and decision/pay criteria.
- A scorecard: segment, decision-maker, workflow recurrence, stated impact, tool/data constraints, budget owner, and quote/prototype interest.

After human approval, use the same script with a small set of independently sourced eligible businesses. The learning goal is not lead volume; it is whether one workflow and buyer recur. Do not accept customer data in this experiment.

## Recommendation

Choose **B** for validation, narrowed to one non-SES segment and one operational workflow. The entry offer should be a **workflow diagnostic plus a fixed-scope prototype proposal**; implementation follows only after a signed scope and explicit approval. The initial differentiation is not a claim of superior technology: it is the combination of narrow problem framing, transparent boundaries, synthetic demo, and owner-level explanation.

Keep A as a second-stage option only if three or more B discovery conversations reveal the same workflow, inputs, outputs, and approval process. Do not pursue C without a separately evidenced product/category thesis and operational plan.

## Risks

- **Compliance:** employer-derived contacts or information could be used inadvertently. Maintain a source log for every contact and exclude any ambiguous source.
- **Credibility:** no client work may reduce confidence. Do not imply the synthetic demo is a client deployment.
- **Scope creep:** "IT担当" may sound broad. The marketing entry point must remain one workflow and explicitly exclude broad integrations, automation, and mobile applications.
- **Privacy/security:** even discovery can elicit personal or commercial data. Request process descriptions, not records; document deletion/handling rules before any sample is received.
- **Segment mismatch:** real estate and nightlife may have distinct operating needs and sensitivities; do not combine them in a single offer.
- **False validation:** favourable feedback is weaker than concrete willingness to schedule a scoped next step or evaluate a price.
- **Capacity:** support expectations and evening/weekend response norms can conflict with the founder's employment.

## Next actions

1. Confirm employer rules and build a contact-origin register; remove all employer-connected contacts from the candidate pool.
2. Choose one segment only using three criteria: compliant reachability, recurring workflow, and owner-level decision access. Record the choice and its evidence.
3. Create the internal offer one-pager, two message variants, scorecard, and fictitious-data demo brief. No publication or outreach.
4. Set pass criteria before outreach approval: e.g., five completed discovery calls, three businesses with the same high-priority workflow, two requests to review a prototype, and one request for a concrete paid-scope discussion. These are validation thresholds, not forecasts.
5. Seek explicit approval separately for any spend, publication, prospect contact, price proposal, data receipt, or use of external AI.

**Medium** that B is the best first marketing hypothesis under the stated constraints. **Low** confidence in any named sector, message performance, channel volume, or willingness to pay until compliant discovery produces evidence.
